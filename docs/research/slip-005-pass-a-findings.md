## F1 — A retainer reads as a monthly reminder. Severity 3

**Evidence.** Asked what happens next month and what an October template change
does to September, the participant answered: *"I'm not quite sure here. But
this is just a monthly reminder right?"*

**Why this is the most serious finding in the session.** Locked beta decision 2
is that native monthly-retainer workflows are more valuable than generic
recurring-task workarounds. That claim is the reason the beta exists. If the
retainer surface reads as a recurring reminder to the person who specified it,
it will read as one to a stranger, and the differentiator is invisible at the
exact moment a prospect decides whether this is another task app.

**Cause.** Two separate gaps, both in the interface rather than the model.

1. The retainer detail view is entirely retrospective. It shows August, July,
   and June, and never states what will happen on September 1 or what will be
   created when it does. The forward promise is the whole product, and it was
   nowhere on screen.
2. The template-history invariant was asserted, not demonstrated. "Template
   edits only affect future cycles" appeared once, as a 12 px caption on the
   creation form that is gone by the time the detail view loads. The detail
   view has no way to edit a template at all, so the second half of the probe
   could not be answered by using the product.

**Resolution.** The detail view now opens with a plain-language statement of
what the next cycle generates and when, including that generation happens once
per cycle regardless of retries, and an explicit contrast with what a reminder
would do instead. Templates are editable from the detail view, and saving an
edit states which cycles are affected and which are not, while the current
cycle continues to render the deliverable set that was generated when it
opened.

## F2 — Top 3 reads as system-chosen. Severity 3

**Evidence.** Asked who chose the three focus items, the participant answered:
*"Slipwell chose the first 2, and i can chose the 3rd one."*

**Why this is severity 3.** The rubric puts any misunderstanding of a domain
invariant at level 3 or above regardless of task completion. Two invariants are
misread here: Top 3 contains at most three *user-chosen* items, and suggestions
must explain why they appear and may never silently replace a user's choices.
The participant completed the task and still formed a false belief about who
controls their own attention list, which is the belief the entire Today surface
depends on.

**Cause.** The section header already said "Chosen by you" and was not
believed, which is the useful part of this finding: a global label does not
override what individual rows imply. The two seeded items carried no authorship
of their own, and because the prototype had no suggestion mechanism anywhere,
there was no visible contrast between "something you chose" and "something
Slipwell proposed". Absent that contrast, pre-existing content reads as
system-generated by default.

**Resolution.** Every focus item now carries its own authorship line stating
that the user added it and when. The open slot is now filled by an explicit
suggestion that is visibly *not* in the list, states the rule that produced it,
and offers both accept and decline. Declining leaves the slot open rather than
substituting something else.

## F3 — Filed work has no visible destination. Severity 2

**Evidence.** *"In the task tab, i don't see anything, i'm not sure if this is
expected."*

Tasks is a documented prototype boundary
([expert audit B5](./slip-005-expert-audit.md)), so this is not a product
defect and must not be counted as a failed task. It is still a real observation:
the participant filed something, went looking for where it landed, found an
empty room, and could not tell whether the emptiness was a boundary or a bug.
"Did my thing save?" is the exact anxiety the capture principle exists to
prevent, and an unlabelled placeholder reintroduces it.

**Resolution.** Placeholder destinations now name what would live there, and
the Tasks placeholder names the task that was just filed and where it currently
appears.

## F4 — Carryover provenance is understood only partially. Severity 2

**Evidence.** Asked how many copies of the July handoff exist and where each
one is, the participant answered: *"two incomplete can get handoff to next
month or just cancel."*

That describes the mechanism correctly and does not answer the question. The
invariant at stake is that a carryover is one linked copy with visible
provenance, and that the original is never moved or deleted. The participant
did not state a false belief, so this is not level 3, but the interface did not
supply the answer either, and a stranger who assumes "carry forward" means
"move" would form exactly the wrong model of their own history.

**Resolution.** Copy on both sides of a carryover now states the count and the
direction of the link explicitly, rather than relying on the reader to infer it
from two cards sitting side by side.

## Passing results worth recording

**T6 and S5, the slipping vocabulary.** Unprompted, the participant defined
slipping as *"it has been slipped or left unattended"*, which is the intended
meaning and is distinct from overdue. This is the weakest kind of evidence,
since the participant named the concept, but it is consistent with the term
surviving into the beta. Pass B decides it.

**T2, source preservation.** *"It cleaned up for me, date and time actually got
transfer to due date."* The cleaning and date extraction were understood. The
probe about what happened to the original words was not answered, so
preservation of the source capture is recorded as untested rather than passing.

**S1 to S3.** Microphone denial, keyboard-only operation, and the 320 px layout
all passed, which confirms the fixes in
[the expert audit](./slip-005-expert-audit.md) hold in use and not just in test.

## What is still unknown after this session

Unchanged from the expert audit, and the reason Pass B exists:

- whether a first-time user understands nothing is filed until they accept;
- whether slipping beats an overdue list in perceived value, rather than merely
  being definable;
- whether the retainer model is understood by someone who did not specify it,
  now that F1 is fixed;
- whether creator-consultant is the right launch identity, which depends on
  [SLIP-002](https://github.com/xuanhieu2611/Slipwell/issues/2).
